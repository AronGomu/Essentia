Shot design, plumbing and copy

Every engine and runtime decision is settled, so the shot list unblocks. Before the questions: uv is verified working on this machine, hashes and C extensions and all, so Round 3's answer holds. Two new problems surfaced. The forced-alignment model you would reach for first is licensed non-commercial, and the card catalog the pipeline wants to read is gitignored generated output, not source of truth. Seventeen questions.

Scope settled -> Engine settled -> Runtime settled -> Resolution settled -> Plumbing -> Shot list -> Overlays -> Palette -> Assets -> Copy -> Tests
1. Where does the pipeline read card fields from?

Round 3 noted that name, rules text, colours, archetype and collector number are all sitting in website/src/generated/catalog.ts. What was not visible then: that file is gitignored, regenerated by release_package.py aggregate, and AGENT.md states cards_mse/ is the only source of truth for card fields. Reading generated output makes the pipeline depend on a website build having run.

2. Which alignment model, given the licence problem?

Round 3 settled on torchaudio forced alignment and named MMS_FA as the likely bundle. It turns out MMS_FA is released under CC-BY-NC 4.0. NonCommercial restricts using the model, not merely redistributing it, and you are producing content for a monetised channel. This needs a different answer than the one you were given.

3. What does the render plan look like?

Python computes the timeline and geometry; the Node renderer draws frames. Whatever passes between them is the seam your unit tests target, since Round 2 settled on testing pure logic rather than pixels. How visible you make that seam decides how debuggable the whole thing is.

4. What does the transcript look like, and what can be overridden?

The transcript is the one thing you hand-write per card, so its ergonomics matter more than anything else in the pipeline. It carries the words for alignment and the act markers, and those two purposes conflict: markers must not reach the aligner as words.

5. What does --preview actually do?

Round 1 settled that --preview exists for iteration, and Round 3 settled that it downgrades fatal errors to warnings. What it does to the render itself is still open, and it is the difference between a 20-second feedback loop and a 3-minute one. Every frame is a browser screenshot, so frame count is the cost.

6. Fallback fractions and transition duration

Markers set the act boundaries. When they are missing, the pipeline falls back to fractions of the narration, and those need real numbers. Transition duration is a separate free parameter: too short and the dip to light reads as a glitch, too long and it eats the pacing.

7. Act 1: what is on screen, and how does it move?

Round 2 settled the plate: artwork at full width over a blurred, darkened, zoomed copy of itself. Open is what else appears and how much the shot moves. This is the scroll-stopping frame, so it carries the most weight per second in the whole video.

8. Act 2: the whip-pan reveal, mechanically

Round 2 settled that act 2 opens with a whip-pan or flash and the card is already in place when the frame resolves. The mechanics are open: direction, how long the blur lasts, whether the artwork stays behind the card, and how big the card lands. The geometry figure gives real numbers to confirm or override.

9. Act 3: framing the textbox zoom

Round 2 settled a constant rect with a slow pan down when rules text is long. The numbers are now known: the textbox lands at 734 x 336 on screen, so filling the width is a 1.47x zoom, and the print master supplies 1244 px of real detail for a 1080 px target. What is open is the framing and the threshold that switches on the pan.

10. What is on the end card?

2.5 seconds, after the narration stops, with a spoken CTA already delivered. It has to name the destinations and make clear the links are in the description, without becoming a wall of text nobody reads before swiping.

11. Caption and watermark placement

This is the tightest constraint in the whole layout. The card bottom lands at y 1535 and Reels chrome starts around y 1500, leaving essentially no clear band beneath the card. Captions have to overlap something. Round 2 settled one to three words, large, centred, hard pop on each change; Round 1 settled a persistent watermark.

12. Where does the palette come from?

Round 1 settled "palette from card art". Scouting found that no colour-extraction code exists anywhere in the repo, so that option means writing new code, while three usable palettes are already sitting in the card data: the archetype accent token, the Magic colour identity, and rarity.

13. Which typeface does what on screen?

Round 3 settled on vendoring Charter plus an OFL sans. What each does in the video is open. Captions and card names have opposite requirements: one is read in motion at a glance, the other is a title.

14. The mse_images rebuild: target size and provenance

The rebuild reuses the existing resize_cover(), so framing is reproduced exactly. What is open is the target size and where the record goes. Current files are a zoo: 316x231 from the current scripts, plus legacy 311x228, 315x231 and 317x233. The stylesheet window is 311x228, so 4x is 1244x912. Nothing lints these dimensions today. Note that book-of-moon-image.png is 745x1040, a whole card rather than an art crop, and will need special handling.

15. The post graphic, in detail

Round 3 settled a 1080x1350 composed graphic plus a bare card PNG as slide two, the same file to X. What goes on the composed graphic is open, and so is the source for the bare card slide: the print master is 1500x2092, which is taller than 1350 and would need resizing either way.

16. Copy templates and the core hashtags

Round 3 settled four files: long caption, short caption for X, YouTube description, and alt text. All of them are deterministic templates over card data. What is open is their actual shape, and the fixed core hashtag set that every post carries.

17. What does the test suite assert?

Round 2 settled the shape: unit-test pure logic, assert output metadata with ffprobe, no pixel comparison. The repo's suite is plain unittest, 19 files, using REPO_ROOT = Path(__file__).resolve().parents[1] and temp dirs, with some tests already invoking external binaries. There is no ruff, black or mypy config anywhere, so style follows the existing scripts rather than a formatter.
