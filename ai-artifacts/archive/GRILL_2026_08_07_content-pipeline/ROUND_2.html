Engine, timing and the three acts

Round 1 settled the contract and the creative shape: one card per command, Python in .script/, render-only with captions burned in, audio-driven three-act montage, zoom into the card's own textbox, palette from art, print-master input. Seventeen decisions are now unblocked. Two surveyed facts change what is possible — read the geometry and resolution panels before answering questions 6, 7 and 11.

1. Which render engine composes the frames?

The single decision the rest of the build hangs off. Round 1 fixed Python in .script/; that still leaves three real ways to make pixels.

2. What may be added to the Python dependency set?

Surveyed fact: the repo installs with pip install --require-hashes -r requirements-dev.lock and currently pins only Pillow. There is no venv and no nix shell. Word-level alignment (question 3) is the expensive one — true forced alignment means PyTorch.

3. How are word timings derived, given you supply the transcript?

You added the transcript as a fourth mandatory input, which is a gift: the text is known, so this is an alignment problem, not a recognition problem. Surveyed fact: ffmpeg's built-in whisper filter gives segment-level timing only; true forced alignment needs PyTorch or an unpackaged pip tool.

4. What do the burned-in captions look like?

Round 1 put them in the safe band. Style decides whether you need per-word timing precision at all — and libass can do all of these natively.

5. What decides where the act boundaries fall?

Round 1 said beats are fractions of audio length. Now that a transcript exists, better options open up — and act boundaries landing on the wrong word is the fastest way for the montage to feel machine-made.

6. Where does act 1's artwork plate come from?

Surveyed fact, and it is a wall: original_images/ has 223 files at 624×624; original_images_hd/ has only 3 files at 1920×1920. The art window cropped out of a 1500×2092 print master is 1244×912. Act 1 is a push-in on artwork, so this decides whether act 1 can be sharp.

7. How does square or landscape art fill a 9:16 frame?

No artwork source is vertical. Something has to give when a 1:1 or 4:3 image meets a 9:16 frame, and the answer sets the visual signature of every video you make.

8. What is the card reveal motion in act 2?

The payoff shot. Round 1 called it "the card settles into frame" — this pins down what that actually means, and how hard the engine has to work.

9. How is the textbox zoom located, and what about long rules text?

Surveyed fact makes the location free: the rules box is always at x 31, y 328, w 311, h 142 on a 375×523 card — 8.3% / 62.7% / 82.9% / 27.2% — and every checked-in card uses that one stylesheet with has_styling: false. The open problem is text that does not stay readable when the box is filled with six lines.

10. What is the transition grammar between the acts?

You asked for light and dark fades specifically. Two cuts in the video, and consistency across a set is what makes them read as a series rather than as three unrelated clips.

11. Which MSE render tier does the pipeline consume?

Surveyed fact you should know before committing to Round 1's answer: renders_print/ currently exists in zero packages. The 1500×2092 print export template is coded but has never been run. MSE re-renders natively at whatever size the export template asks for — it is not upscaling — and the template is the only lever, since the CLI takes no resolution flag.

12. Which font files does the pipeline vendor?

Surveyed fact: DESIGN.md asks for Charter and Aptos. Neither is installed here, and Aptos is Microsoft proprietary — the font file cannot legally be redistributed. Bitstream Charter is permissively licensed and can be vendored. ffmpeg and libass need a file path, not a CSS stack, so "reuse DESIGN.md" needs a concrete resolution.

13. Does the logo block the build?

Round 1 chose a commissioned logo mark consumed as an asset. No logo exists today, and the watermark plus end card both want one.

14. Where do the four inputs live, and how are they named?

You now have four inputs, but the rules text and the card image are already machine-readable — the catalog at website/src/generated/catalog.ts carries name, ruleText, colors, archetype and image paths per card id. So some of your "inputs" may not need to be inputs at all.

15. Where do outputs land, and do they go into git?

A short is tens of megabytes. The repo already enforces hard size limits on renders (2 MiB per set, 512 KiB per card), so it clearly cares about weight. Round 1 also needs the caption and description text emitted somewhere you can paste from.

16. How is the pipeline verified?

This repo runs unittest, a style linter and a package validator on every change. A media pipeline resists normal testing — pixel output is huge and encoders are not bit-reproducible across versions.

17. What is the music policy, and is there sound design?

Round 1 chose a ducked music bed and you will source a copyright-free track. That leaves how many tracks exist, and whether the two act transitions get a sound.
